# OpenCoast moderation policy (initial)

## Review each claim as a claim

The map describes what the submitted evidence supports. Moderators verify a proposal's location, exact geometry, jurisdiction, wording, conditions, and source links before publication. A report of being moved on by a business is an observation; it is not by itself proof of the legal boundary or rights. A lease or concession may impose conditions without making the entire coast inaccessible. Documented legal rights and observed obstruction can be recorded separately.

No coast is colored because of a countrywide rule alone. A submitted boundary remains **approximate** until an authoritative geometry is obtained and separately checked. A beach area does not establish a land access route. Review a route as its own record.

## Evidence labels

- **Document backed:** At least one linked official source was checked against the claim and geometry. Quote or summarize its relevant provision in the moderator explanation. Explain uncertain boundaries and exceptions.
- **Community reviewed, no official source verified:** A moderator found the description useful enough to display but no official source was verified. Keep the visible label prominent. Do not state a legal conclusion as certain.

Access status (`allowed`, `conditional`, `restricted`, `disputed`, `unknown`) is separate from evidence strength. Use **conditional** for documented limits, **disputed** when credible evidence conflicts, and **unknown** where the actual rule cannot be established. Rejection does not mean access is forbidden.

## Review steps

1. Read the full description and inspect the drawn feature at a useful zoom. Check that the shape does not cover unrelated land or water.
2. Open source links and identify the issuing body, date, scope, and any amendment or expiry. Record what was actually checked.
3. Inspect private uploads for relevance, authenticity cues, faces, names, location metadata, and permission to publish. Do not publish raw originals. The app can produce a metadata-stripped JPEG copy of a consented image, but the moderator must also check the visible content.
4. Check nearby published records and pending proposals for overlap or conflict. Ask for clarification if details are missing.
5. Edit wording for precision when needed, with a written audit reason. Choose the access status and evidence label separately.
6. Write a short public explanation of why the claim is displayed and what remains uncertain. Select public images only after the checks above. Approve, reject with a reason, or request clarification.

For early Montenegro entries and any contested claim, seek a second independent moderator to check the legal source and geometry before promotion. Pause or label a challenged record as disputed while it is rechecked. Keep source and review dates current; an edit date alone does not imply new legal review.

## Privacy and conflicts

Never publish receipt tokens, contributor identity, private messages, raw documents, or unapproved photos. Do not use moderation to retaliate against a company or individual. Disclose a personal stake in a dispute and ask another moderator to review it. Avoid naming a person or alleging a crime unless the public source directly supports the statement and publication is necessary to explain access.
